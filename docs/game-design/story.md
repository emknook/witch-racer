# Story and setting

## Confirmed narrative direction

The protagonist begins as an amateur witch who enjoys racing her broom and experimenting in her shed, possibly alongside a friend. Her first step into organized competition could be a practice run or local event where a coach/scout notices her talent; that encounter is **proposed**, not confirmed.

The campaign grows from grassroots competition through increasingly prestigious races toward international championships, like an athlete developing from local competition to the world stage. The protagonist is **not one of the eight established witches**. A permanent racing club or school is not part of the confirmed opening.

Unknown to the racers, championship organizers have long used the popularity of the sport to improve each generation's speed and skill. Their hidden purpose is to prepare witches to survive Walpurgisnacht and recapture Walpurgis. Rumours and clues accumulate throughout the championships; the connection becomes clear at roughly **70–80% of story-track progression**. This is a pacing target, not a fixed number of tracks.

The finale is a multi-stage race in which the witches construct a magical sealing circle by following its tracks. All eight established witches participate, with AI filling their roles in singleplayer. See [the final-race design](walpurgisnacht-final-race.md).

## Tone and storytelling scope

Begin friendly and playful, emphasizing experimentation, racing ambition, rivals and friendships. Gradually introduce unease without revealing the true purpose of the championships too early. Keep storytelling economical: brief scenes between competitions, character interactions and environmental details; racing remains the main activity.

## Recovered Walpurgisnacht setting

Earlier lore describes an ancient creature escaping confinement, magical routes and Wardens restoring thirteen seals. These details remain **working lore**, not all confirmed canon. See [Walpurgisnacht lore](walpurgisnacht.md). The old *Grand Broom Prix* mishap/trophy storyline is superseded and should not be treated as an alternative active premise.

## Open narrative decisions

- Who the protagonist and her friend are, and whether the friend becomes an engineer, racer or both.
- Who first recognizes the protagonist's talent, and how she enters her first competition.
- Identities and relationships of the eight established racers.
- The organizers' identity, how the secret is revealed and how the characters respond.
- Precise capture mechanics, event consequences and the balance of humor and tension.
