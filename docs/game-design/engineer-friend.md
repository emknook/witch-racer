# Engineer friend — character exploration

> **Proposal for review, not canon.** The protagonist is a new story character, separate from the eight selectable racers. These notes develop her engineer friend without prematurely fixing names, designs, or gameplay systems.

## The central friendship

The protagonist is an energetic, impulsive magical runner who loves the sensation of movement, speed, experimentation, and taking the next corner just a little faster. **Energy reference:** Tracer's playful momentum and confidence, not her exact mannerisms, voice, or design.

Her friend is **equally excited, but for a different reason**: she is utterly captivated by the engineering of enchanted brooms, spell mechanisms, and the beautiful absurdity of making magic *work*. **Enthusiasm reference:** the protagonist of *Knight's & Magic* and his obsessive delight in mecha engineering, translated into magical craftsmanship; not a character copy.

**Dynamic:** “I want to try it!” meets “I NEED to know how you made it do that!” Neither is the sensible, permanently exasperated straight woman. They amplify each other's excitement, with different priorities.

## Engineer personality anchors

- **Craft-first fascination:** She notices the suspension rune, bristle alignment, or impossible energy transfer before noticing the race winner.
- **Specific, infectious enthusiasm:** Can talk for minutes about a tiny improvement; genuinely wants others to appreciate why it matters.
- **Hands-on curiosity:** Always testing, dismantling, annotating, rebuilding, and keeping failed prototypes.
- **Pride without infallibility:** Skilled and inventive, but her beautiful solutions sometimes overcomplicate things. Mistakes have consequences and teach her something.
- **Supportive agency:** She cares about her friend as a person, not merely as a test pilot. She has her own ambitions and boundaries.
- **Not a generic gadget vendor:** Her craft obsession drives her decisions, relationships, and story arc.

## Three possible interpretations

| Direction | Signature | Strength | Risk to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rune mechanic** | Inscribes and tunes magical circuits, sees brooms as elegant spell systems | Distinct visual language: chalk diagrams, glowing seals, field notebooks | Too much exposition or magic-as-computer jargon |
| **Broomwright tinkerer** | Loves materials, balance, bristles, handles, and the feel of a handmade machine | Strong workshop identity and tactile PSX-era props | Becoming a mundane mechanic with magic paint |
| **Experimental enchantress** | Makes delightfully improbable prototypes by combining spells in new ways | Comedy, surprise, and expressive animations | Random inventions without consistent rules |

**Recommended blend:** Broomwright's tactile craft + rune mechanic's rigorous curiosity, with the experimental enchantress's occasional gleeful breakthroughs.

## Introduction: the shed

The protagonist has been trying to make her broom faster in a small shed, possibly with the engineer friend sharing the workspace. A practice run or local competition provides an achievable first goal. A coach notices the protagonist's potential after seeing her fly; this is an invitation into bigger races, **not** a local-club membership premise.

Possible opening beats:
1. The protagonist rushes into the shed, ecstatic about a risky maneuver she almost landed.
2. The engineer ignores the boast for half a second because she has spotted a fascinating, unexpected wear pattern on the broom.
3. They excitedly talk over each other, then realize the maneuver and the engineering discovery are connected.
4. They make one *small, understandable* change and test it in a practice run.
5. The coach notices the flying, while the engineer is already sketching the next iteration.

The engineer is an important recurring character, **not automatically a ninth selectable racer**. Whether she races occasionally is an open decision.

## Dialogue tone sketches (original examples)

- **Runner:** “Did you see that turn? I nearly clipped the roof!”
  **Engineer:** “YES! And the binding rune held at that angle? Wait. Do it again. No—let me reinforce it first!”
- **Engineer:** “This is not just a faster broom. Listen to the way the enchantment settles when you lean.”
  **Runner:** “I hear wind and a terrible idea. I love it.”
- **Runner:** “Race starts in ten minutes.”
  **Engineer:** “Perfect! That gives me nine minutes to finish the stabilizer.”

## Visual and animation hooks

- Practical work clothes, pockets, tied-back sleeves, protective goggles; silhouettes should read clearly in low-poly/PSX style.
- Tool roll, rune-etched calipers, messy diagrams, prototype broom parts, scorch marks, repair patches.
- Animations: excitedly leaning over a mechanism, quick sketching, hands moving as she explains, triumphant tiny fist pump after a successful test.
- Avoid making her visual identity depend only on goggles or making her indistinguishable from a playable racer.

## Narrative role and boundaries

The engineer can provide a human reason to revisit the shed between races, comment on new course hazards, and help frame broom upgrades **if** an upgrade mechanic is eventually approved. Do not assume upgrade trees, stat bonuses, crafting minigames, or a mandatory tutorial character yet.

A potential arc: she begins by pursuing the perfect machine; watching her friend race teaches her to design for a living, improvising pilot rather than an idealized test scenario. In return, the runner learns to respect preparation and craft.

## Questions for review

1. Should she be a dedicated non-playable engineer, or occasionally race herself?
2. Which craft identity feels most compelling: rune mechanic, broomwright, or experimental enchantress?
3. Is the shed jointly owned, or does one friend regularly invade the other's workspace?
4. How much of the engineer's technical passion should be shown through optional dialogue versus visible props and short scenes?
5. Do we want a rivalry with another inventor, or keep her ambition centered on building something extraordinary with her friend?

## Continuity

- The **story protagonist is separate from the eight selectable racers**; do not quietly replace or rename one of the eight.
- The starting point is friendly experimentation and a practice run/local competition, not an established racing club.
- Walpurgisnacht lore and the later pursuit remain separate narrative layers to connect once the opening and friendship are approved.
- Character references are shorthand for *energy and motivation*, not a license to copy protected designs or dialogue.
