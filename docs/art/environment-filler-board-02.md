# Witch Racer — environment-filler board 02

**Purpose:** refine the environment asset direction around the Drummyfish Blender model the project selected as its strongest style reference. This board focuses on *environment filling*: small repeated assets that make a route feel inhabited and readable without obscuring the racing line.

**Working direction:** stylized, hand-painted low-poly pieces with simple, recognizable silhouettes and a small number of strong color/material areas. Use this as a target to test in Blender and Godot, not as a final palette or rendering lock.

The image previews below are from Drummyfish’s *RPG Item Collection 3*. The collection is published as **CC0 1.0**. Its creator asks for no attribution, but this board credits them as a courtesy. The preview images are hosted by BlendSwap; the [OpenGameArt source page](https://opengameart.org/content/rpg-item-collection-3) records the collection author and license.

## Witch Racer takeaways

- **Prioritize silhouette:** trees, rocks, signs and landmarks should read quickly from a fast, elevated camera.
- **Use texture as an accent:** painted surfaces can add warmth and material character; keep value contrast clear enough that track edges remain visible.
- **Fill in layers:** near-route props can be more distinct, with simpler repeated silhouettes farther away.
- **Make routes legible:** vary terrain and landmarks to help the player anticipate curves, while keeping decoration outside the drivable space.
- **Prototype as a kit:** build a small Blender test set—one ground tile, one tree, one rock and one sign—then check scale, lighting, texture filtering and distance in Godot before expanding.
